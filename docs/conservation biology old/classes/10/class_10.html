  • The Whittaker (1975) formula: \(\beta=(S/\alpha)-1\), where \(S\) is the total number of species in the area, and \(\alpha\) is the average number of species in the area. \(\beta\) diversity gives us an index of species turnover along a geographic gradient.

  • We can calculate the beta index for dataset 1

\[ \beta=\frac{5}{(5+3+3+4+5)/5}-1=0.25 \]

Background rate of extinction

  • A cohort of 825 amphibian species has been described in the last century and has accumulated 74215 species-years, meaning that one species has been known, on average, for 90 years. Since their description, 28 have gone extinct. Can you estimate the extinction rate?

\[ 28:74215=x:10^6 \]

. . .

\[ x = \frac{28}{74215}*10^6 \approx 377 \]

Probability of a gamete going to fixation

  • We work with a diploid population with \(n = 9\). Individuals are typed at a bi-allelic locus and the frequency of allele \(A_1\) is \(p = 0.31\). What is the probability of losing allele \(A_1\) in one generation of sampling to recreate a population of the same size?

\[ P(fixation \ A_2)=(1-p)^{2n} \]

. . .

\[ P(fixation \ A_2)=(1-0.31)^{18} = 0.00125 \]

Minimal loss of genetic diversity

  • You are in charge of a program to maintaining the genetic variability of flying foxes population to a level of 0.0075. After 80 generations of only genetic drift acting on the population you measure heterozygosity and found that it has dropped to 0.0071. What has been the effective population size during this time?

  • To determine the smallest fully outbreeding population size (N) that maintained heterozygosity over this period of time under genetic drift, we use the formula for the decline in heterozygosity due to drift: \(H_{t}=H_0(1-\frac{1}{2N})^t\)

    • \(H_0 = 0.0075\) is the initial heterozygosity

    • \(H_t = 0.0071\) final heterozigosity

    • \(t\) = 80 generations

    • \(N\) = is the population size we want to solve for

\[ 0.0071 = 0.0075*(1-\frac{1}{2N})^{80} \]

Estimating inbreeding using pedigree

\[ F_P = ((\frac{1}{2})^3*(1+0))+((\frac{1}{2})^3*(1+0)) \]

\[ F_P = \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{4} = 0.25 \]

\[ F_P = ((\frac{1}{2})^5*(1+0))+((\frac{1}{2})^5*(1+0))+((\frac{1}{2})^5*(1+0))+((\frac{1}{2})^5*(1+0)) \]

\[ F_P = \frac{1}{32} + \frac{1}{32} + \frac{1}{32} + \frac{1}{32} = \frac{1}{8} = 0.125 \]
